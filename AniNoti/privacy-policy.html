AniNoti privacy policy

Last updated 21 August 2026

The short version. AniNoti has no accounts and no sign-in. The series you follow, the searches you run and your reminder settings are stored on your phone, not on our servers. The Android version collects anonymous usage and crash statistics to find and fix bugs; the iOS version collects nothing of its own. The app carries ads, and Google's ad system uses your device's advertising identifier to choose them — that is the one thing here that follows you between apps, it is covered in full below, and nothing about what you do in AniNoti is used to pick an ad. We do not sell your data.

Who this is about

This policy covers the AniNoti mobile app on Android and iOS. In it, "we" means the developer of AniNoti and "you" means the person using the app.

What stays on your device

The following never leaves your phone, is never transmitted to us, and is deleted when you uninstall the app:

  • The anime series you follow
  • Your reminder settings — whether reminders are on, how far in advance, and your quiet hours
  • Which reminders have already been delivered, so the app does not repeat them
  • A cached copy of everything the app has shown you — the airing schedule and calendar, series pages, cast and staff, reviews, and the results of searches and of the browse rails — so the screens still open without a connection
  • The searches you have run most recently, so that returning to Search does not show you an empty screen and so the app can refresh those results in the background
  • Whether you have completed onboarding, and whether you have been asked for notification permission

The cache is a copy of AniList's public catalogue, held on your phone so the app is usable on a train. It is not a record of you kept anywhere we can read, and clearing the app's storage or uninstalling removes it.

On Android, this data is included in Android's Auto Backup if you have device backup switched on. That backup goes to your own Google account, is governed by Google's privacy policy, and is not accessible to us. You can turn it off in your device settings. On iOS the equivalent is your iCloud device backup.

Settings → Your data can turn your followed list into a short code on your clipboard, so you can move it to another device yourself. That happens only when you tap Export, the code contains only the public catalogue numbers of the series you follow, and it is never sent to us — where you paste it from there is up to you.

What we collect

Analytics and crash reporting are Android only. The iOS build has no analytics or crash SDK linked into it. Advertising applies to both. AniNoti shows ads on iPhone and Android alike, and that is covered in its own section below.

The Android build uses Google Firebase for anonymous analytics and crash reporting. This is what it collects and why.

DataWhy
App usage events — which series and episode pages were opened, identified by their public AniList ID; how many results a search returned; whether a schedule load was answered from the network or from the cache on your device To understand which parts of the app are used and which are failing. The text you type into Search is never sent — only the number of results it produced
Crash reports and error diagnostics — the stack trace, the app version, the device model and OS version To find and fix crashes
A randomly generated app-instance identifier, plus approximate country derived from your IP address Assigned by Firebase so that events from one install can be counted once. It is reset when you reinstall the app and is not linked to you

This data is not linked to your identity. We do not ask for your name, email address, phone number or location, and there is no account to attach anything to. The advertising identifier is a separate matter, covered in the advertising section below.

Firebase processes this data on our behalf as described in the Firebase privacy documentation. Analytics data is retained by Firebase for up to 14 months; crash reports for up to 90 days.

Turning it off

On Android you can stop analytics collection at the OS level by revoking the app's usage-data sharing in Settings → Google → Ads / Usage & diagnostics. Uninstalling the app stops all collection and deletes everything held on the device.

Advertising

AniNoti is free, and ads are how it pays for itself. They are set into the Airing, Following, Search and Alerts lists as slots between the rows, each one framed and captioned Sponsored. A list carries at most three, the first one no earlier than the seventh row, so an ad is always scrolled to rather than landed on. There are no full-screen ads, no ads on launch, and no ads during onboarding, in Settings, on the calendar or on a series page. The ads are supplied by Google AdMob.

What Google collects, and what it is for

DataWhy
Your device's advertising identifier — the Advertising ID on Android, the IDFA on iOS To choose which ads to show you and to avoid showing the same one repeatedly. You can reset it, or switch it off entirely, in your device settings
Which ads were shown to you and which you interacted with To measure whether an ad was seen, and to bill the advertiser
Approximate location, derived from your IP address To show ads that are legal and relevant in your country. This is not your device's location: AniNoti never asks for location permission and has no access to it
General device information — model, OS version, screen size, language To render the ad correctly and to select an appropriate one

This is the one place where data leaves the app and is linked to an identifier that follows you between apps. Google may combine it with data from other apps and sites in order to personalise what you are shown. That is what "tracking" means in Apple's App Tracking Transparency prompt and in the App Store privacy label, and it is why both say yes.

What is never sent. Nothing about what you do in AniNoti reaches Google's ad systems. Which series you follow, what you search for, what you read, when your reminders are set for — none of it is shared, and none of it is used to choose an ad. The ad slot knows only that it is a slot on a screen.

Your choice, and how to change it

Before any ad is requested, AniNoti asks Google's consent tool whether your country requires your permission — the EEA and the UK do. Where it does, you are shown a consent form and no ad is requested until you have answered it. If you decline, the app does not fall back to less personal ads; it shows none at all.

You can change your mind at any time:

  • In the app — Settings → About → Ad privacy options reopens the same consent form. The row appears only where your country's rules require it, because elsewhere there is no form to reopen.
  • On iOS — Settings → Privacy & Security → Tracking withdraws permission to use the IDFA, for this app or for all of them.
  • On Android — Settings → Google → Ads lets you delete the advertising ID entirely, which stops personalisation across every app on the device.

Google processes this data as an independent controller, under Google's own privacy policy. AniNoti receives no advertising data back: we see aggregate earnings and nothing about who saw what.

Who we send requests to

AniList

All anime data — schedules, titles, synopses, cover art, cast, scores and reviews — comes from the AniList API. When you open a screen or run a search, the app requests that data from AniList's servers. Those requests are anonymous: they carry no account, no identifier and nothing about what you follow. AniList receives the request itself and your IP address, as any web server does, and handles it under AniList's own terms and privacy policy. AniNoti is not affiliated with AniList.

Trailers and streaming links

Series pages can play a trailer hosted by YouTube or Dailymotion, embedded in the app. Playing a trailer loads content from those services, which set their own cookies and collect their own data under Google's and Dailymotion's policies. Nothing loads from them until you tap play.

"Where to watch" links open your browser or the streaming service's own app. Once you leave AniNoti, that service's privacy policy applies, not this one.

Cover images

Cover artwork is loaded directly from AniList's image servers, and is cached on your device so it does not have to be fetched twice.

Notifications

Episode reminders are scheduled and delivered entirely on your device by the operating system. No reminder is sent from a server, and we are never told that one was delivered or opened. If you decline the notification permission, the rest of the app carries on working.

Children

AniNoti is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect data from them. Since there are no accounts, there is no personal information for a child to give us. The app is rated 13+ on both stores, and the ads it shows are not from a child-directed programme — which is another reason it should not be installed for a younger child.

Your rights

Because we hold no account and no identifier that points at you, we generally cannot locate, export or delete "your" data on request — there is nothing tied to you to find. What you can do at any time:

  • Delete everything held on the device by uninstalling the app.
  • Stop future collection using the OS controls described above.
  • Ask us a question using the contact address below. If you are in the UK, EU or another region with data-protection rights, and you can identify the data in question, we will do what we can to help.

Changes

If this policy changes in a way that affects what is collected, the updated version will be published at this address with a new date at the top, and the change will be described in the app's release notes.
